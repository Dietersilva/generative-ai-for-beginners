Intolerance (1916)

← Retour

Griffith built this Babylon set to a scale nothing in film had attempted before -- walls and towering statuary reportedly visible for miles across Hollywood, populated with some 3,000 extras for its crowd scenes, standing for years afterward as a local landmark before it was finally torn down. He made the film partly as his answer to the firestorm of criticism over his previous picture, The Birth of a Nation: an epic about the destructive cost of intolerance across four different eras of history, crosscut together in a way virtually no one had tried at feature length before. It was a costly commercial failure on release and contributed to Griffith's later financial troubles, but its editing techniques went on to influence nearly every filmmaker who saw it.
