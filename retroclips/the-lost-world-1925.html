The Lost World (1925)

← Retour

Willis O'Brien's stop-motion effects here were the first time mass audiences had seen dinosaurs move and interact with a live-action world at this scale, and the techniques he refined -- posable armature models, incremental frame-by-frame animation, miniatures matted into real footage -- became the direct blueprint for his own King Kong eight years later. Three years before the film even opened, Arthur Conan Doyle previewed O'Brien's test reel -- a Triceratops family, an Allosaurus attack, a Stegosaurus -- for a room of magicians that included Harry Houdini, coyly calling the creatures "psychic" and letting the room debate whether they'd just watched something real. In 1998 the Library of Congress added the finished film to the National Film Registry.
